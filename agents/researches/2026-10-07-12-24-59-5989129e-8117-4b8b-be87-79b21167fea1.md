# Registration identity and deterministic lifecycle testing

A callback pointer is not a lifetime guarantee. A copied callback can outlive its
owner even when the registry entry is removed. Returning a registration identity
instead allows every invocation to verify that the same registration still exists.
A monotonically increasing identity prevents an old handle from affecting a new
registration that reuses the same command name.

Synchronous callbacks can still reenter lifecycle code. The registry therefore
pins the entry before validation, enablement and invocation. Disposal reports
in-use while the pin is held. Dispatch copies the callback and looks up its
identity again after user code, avoiding a pointer into a map that may grow.
Shutdown closes admission first, then waits for callback unwinding before release.

Deterministic time replaces sleeps: a fake operation reports Pending before its
ready time, succeeds after advancing a manual clock, and rejects cancelled or
expired requests. Tests can inspect ordering and resource ownership without
launching a child or opening a window. Exhaustive allocator failures complement
happy-path tests by checking cleanup after every possible allocation failure.

These are explanations of observable implementation and test behavior. No model
attention, activations, logits or hidden reasoning were captured or inferred.
Reproduce the checks with `zig build check` and its ReleaseSafe variant.
